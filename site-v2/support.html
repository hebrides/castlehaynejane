    Why We're Doing This

    Good land deserves a working farm — so that's what we're building.

    Cape Fear riverfront acreage doesn't stay undeveloped by accident. We've had offers. We said no. This land has been in our family for generations, and the best use we know for it isn't a subdivision — it's orchards, festivals, a campsite along the river, and neighbors who come back year after year.

    It started the same way it always has on this land: good soil, good neighbors, and good music. The WWOOF volunteers, the Old Time music gatherings, a grandmother's house still serving as the homestead — all of that came before any business plan. Everything we're building grows out of what was already here.

    We're organized as a for-profit LLC because a farm that can't pay its own way doesn't survive — and we intend to survive. The U.S. organic market topped $67.6 billion in 2022 and keeps climbing. Our location in the Wilmington corridor — beach tourism, a major university, a nationally recognized restaurant scene, a growing retirement community — gives us a natural audience that's already looking for exactly this. Profitable enough to last; rooted enough to matter.

    The Farm Start Up Fundraiser bridges the gap between where we are now and where we open. Barns, stages, orchards, campsite clearing — infrastructure that turns 26 acres of Cape Fear riverfront into a working farm and gathering place that can support itself. Our operations lead brought this model from a California organic winery. Our agricultural advisor is a CCOF-certified organic inspector with a graduate degree in ecological horticulture from UC Santa Cruz. Jane is an AirBnB Superhost already running five units on this property. We've hosted WWOOF volunteers through the entire build-out. This isn't a concept — it's already moving.
